## Drone control after changing ships and lost-drone recovery, November 6, 2026

The maintainer reported stationary drones, unresponsive commands, and drones appearing under the pilot's control after docking, boarding another ship and undocking. Read-only inspection found five saved drones with no controller, no target and zero velocity. Build 3662168 keeps a drone in its panel when the control row identifies either the current character as owner or the current ship as controller. Its reconnect menu searches visible owned drone objects whose IDs are **absent** from that control table. Eden had included released drones in snapshot control rows and continued sending the physical owner in live updates. This explained both stale panel entries and the inability to reconnect. The inspection did not establish a second failure to advance freshly launched drones on the server.

The physical drone remains visible at its saved position with its original item ID and owner. Snapshots omit uncontrolled drones from `droneState`. Live release removes the control row without removing the space object, clears controller fields on the slim, and stops the drone at its saved position. A new ship can explicitly reconnect, subject to skill, drone count and bandwidth. A previously released save needs no migration. In Eden the reconnect grid is the 250 km observer visibility range.

A fresh group launches outside the hull and receives its initial orbit, target, range and speed. The server continues to integrate motion each tick. An unchanged course now runs between updates on the client, while a change of target, follow/orbit mode, distance or speed issues a new order. The owner's contacts include their controlled drones beyond observer range. An attack requires a locked NPC within the ship's control range when issued. Pursuit continues if the lock or that initial range is lost. Recall remains available while controlled. Control ends beyond 500 km from the host ship, or through the existing warp, docking, travel, destruction and disconnect paths. See [EVE University drone behavior](https://wiki.eveuniversity.org/Drones#Control_range) and the [Carbon Destiny 27ee40b free-ball and orbit format](https://github.com/carbonengine/destiny/blob/27ee40b38a563d29054b32f64c3ebc87fabaec1f/src/Thunkers.cpp).

Build 3662168 uses `entity.CmdAbandonDrone(ids)`, `ship.ScoopDrone(ids)` for the drone bay and `ship.Scoop(id)` for cargo. Abandon releases selected drones in place and frees their bandwidth. Either pilot may scoop an uncontrolled drone within 2,500 m measured between surfaces, with sufficient space in the destination hold. The transaction changes both ownership records and the item's location together, retains individual damage and prevents competing scoops from duplicating it. Scoop is independent of the launch hull and piloting skills. Abandon and scoop-to-bay return the native empty success dictionary. Scoop-to-cargo returns None. See [abandoned drones](https://wiki.eveuniversity.org/Drones#Abandoned_drones).

## Launch inventory and normal-undock synchronization, November 6, 2026

The maintainer reported stationary drones, fewer than five space-panel entries and apparent bay duplication after launching. The screenshot shows **five overview contacts**, **Drones in Space (4/5)** and the original **Hammerhead II (5)** bay stack. Read-only inspection found five saved individual drones in space, rather than five additional saved bay units. Their controller IDs had been released. The original tests checked saved inventory and drone motion with an explicitly saved system ID; they did not establish native cache compatibility or the normal-undock path.

Two defects produced those symptoms. Launching a complete unassembled stack reused its original ID for the final singleton, then emitted an `OnItemChange` containing `ixQuantity` while the new physical DBRow quantity was **-1**. Pinned `invCache.OnItemChange` rejects that combination with **OnItemChange in invCache: negative qty**. The cached bay stack therefore survived. Native `dronesUtil.GetDroneIDsInSpace` excludes IDs still present in the bay cache, hiding the fifth ID from the panel even though its ball existed. Separately, ordinary undocking persists a null `WorldPosition.SystemId`, inheriting the station's system. Launch resolved that correctly, but continuing control and drone/NPC orders compared the nullable field directly against real system IDs. The next owner tick released every drone, leaving zero velocity and rejecting subsequent orders.

Launch now splits unassembled bay contents into new singleton IDs and reduces or removes the original ordinary stack atomically. Full-stack removal uses the existing graveyard notification path, avoiding the invalid assembly/quantity transition. Already assembled individuals retain their identities and damage across repeated launch/recall. `PilotDroneStore.CurrentSystem` resolves explicit travel positions and inherited station systems consistently for launch, ongoing control, attack orders and reconnect. Ownership, skill, count, bandwidth, range and wrong-system rejection remain enforced. `DroneHandler.Advance` also refreshes visible contacts for observers without owned drones, once per second, before deciding whether that connection must advance its own drones; idle observers now discover launches, movement and removals without issuing a request.

An automated audit fed the emitted test notifications into the actual pinned `invCache.OnItemChange`, `invCacheContainer.OnItemChange`, `_ProcessItemChange` and `dronesUtil.GetDroneIDsInSpace` code objects, with DBRow/UI/service dependencies stubbed. Before the correction, a full five-drone launch reproduced **bay 5 / space panel 4** and the negative-quantity exception. Corrected five-drone events produced **bay 0 / space panel 5**, with no exception. The one-drone case produced **0 / 1**, and launching five from seven medium drones produced **2 / 5**. This is automated callback execution, not native gameplay acceptance.

Successful replies map each original stack ID to a Python **list** of launched individual IDs. Eden permits personal ownership only. A whole request validates before mutation; a rejected selection does not partially launch or alter its stacks.

### Rules, combat and lifetime

Launch requires real owned bay contents, an in-space living Brutix, the drone's trained prerequisites, enough effective ship bandwidth and at most **min(Drones level, 5)** controlled drones. Selected quantities split into durable singleton identities; the final selected unit may retain its original stack identity. Repeated/concurrent requests cannot duplicate items or exceed limits. Recall returns those same individual IDs rather than creating replacement stacks. Insufficient bay capacity leaves the drone in space. Losing skills or effective bandwidth releases excess drones without moving or deleting them.

Light/Medium Drone Operation supplies 5% damage per level for its size; Drone Interfacing supplies 10%. A racial specialization supplies 2% only when that drone requires it. Drone Sharpshooting modifies optimal, Drone Navigation modifies chase speed and Drone Durability modifies all three HP capacities by their pinned 5%-per-level bonuses. Control range is 20 km plus 5 km per Drone Avionics level and 3 km per Advanced Drone Avionics level. Manual attack requires a locked live NPC and control-range validation; warping NPCs reject new orders and cancel existing drone attacks; accepted drones then maintain their own chase/orbit and firing intervals. Return-and-orbit cancels that attack. Passive drones wait for orders; aggressive drones may defend against NPCs that fire on their controlling ship after the last explicit order. Focus fire chooses a common attacker; disabled focus fire permits distributing drones across attackers. These target-selection policies are Eden's initial behavior, not a claim about Tranquility's private AI.

Drone motion reuses the existing Carbon-derived `OrbitCourse` and the free-ball mass/agility time constant. It distinguishes chase and orbit speeds in both authoritative movement and client orders. Reference: Carbon Destiny **27ee40b38a563d29054b32f64c3ebc87fabaec1f**, [Ballpark.cpp EvolveOldStyleOrbit](https://github.com/carbonengine/destiny/blob/27ee40b38a563d29054b32f64c3ebc87fabaec1f/src/Ballpark.cpp) and [Thunkers.cpp WriteBallToStream](https://github.com/carbonengine/destiny/blob/27ee40b38a563d29054b32f64c3ebc87fabaec1f/src/Thunkers.cpp). Free-ball Follow/Orbit snapshots retain follow ID/range and explicit mass, velocity, agility and speed fraction. See the [Carbon license](../carbon-license.md).

Turret-style drone shots use catalog tracking, signature and range with Eden's existing accuracy/quality and layered resistance calculations. Saved per-drone deadlines prevent duplicate cycles. NPC damage, hostility, kill history, faction/security changes, bounty/tax and wreck creation share the existing settlement transaction. Competing drones cannot settle a kill twice. Native combat messages and timed effects reach the owner and observers. NPC turrets can retaliate against attacking drones; an initial 25% target-selection opportunity shares the NPC's existing weapon deadline, with real shield/armor/structure damage and permanent drone loss. NPC missiles still target the controlling ship. The resident NPC fast path falls back to the same transactional route while drones are controlled, preventing it from bypassing retaliation.

Schema **85** adds `WorldDrone`, `WorldDroneSettings` and the last NPC weapon target, without granting drones, skills, ISK or health. Position, individual damage, paid deadlines and preferences persist. Ordinary one-second owner updates advance movement/combat; duplicate callbacks and delayed/offline intervals do not replay missed cycles. Warp initiation, docking, gate/GM travel, ship loss and disconnect release control at the saved drone position. Downtime also releases control, clears targets/velocity and performs no offline combat. Reconnect requires owned supported drones in the current system and control range, with current skills/count/bandwidth checks. No transition teleports drones back into the bay or refunds losses.
